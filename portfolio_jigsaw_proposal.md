# Proposed Portfolio Recomposition: **Measured Image Field**

## What I understand

You do **not** want a decorative “box” around every photograph, even if the decoration is cleaner or more colorful. You want the photographs to feel current in the way a strong fashion or design studio portfolio feels current: an intentional composition, restrained graphic cues, and a layout whose energy comes from the relationship between images rather than a generic card treatment.

You also want a jigsaw-like layout, but **not** a random collage. The current official sequence—Portfolio 01 through 54, supplied in eighteen three-image groups—must remain legible and nearly unchanged. The current image widths and breathing room should remain close to their present proportions. The correct solution is therefore a **controlled broken grid**, not masonry and not a freeform mood board.

## Research-led direction

Contemporary portfolio references consistently separate the presentation surface from the interaction. Graphéine’s Awwwards-featured portfolio emphasizes a direct image index and rollover behavior rather than decorative image cards.[1] The Hotel Jägerhof reference describes a “broken grid editorial layout” through controlled off-grid, asymmetric, magazine-like composition.[2] Format’s fashion-portfolio survey points to staggered, negative-space-led layouts as a way to maintain image priority while giving a collection a distinctive visual rhythm.[3]

> The proposal takes the structural restraint of an archive, the compositional rhythm of an editorial spread, and the simple interaction clarity of a contemporary studio site. It does **not** copy any referenced site’s imagery, visual identity, or layout.

## Proposed desktop behavior

The Portfolio becomes a **12-column image field**. It has no visible card boxes, warm-paper mats, inner rules, or shadows. Each image sits directly on the limestone background with only two short, offset registration strokes: **orange at the upper-left** and **cobalt at the lower-right**. These remain outside the image edges, so they read as editorial marks in the page field—not as a frame around a card.

The current preview dimensions remain essentially intact: portrait images use their current narrow portrait measure, landscape images retain their current 20%-larger landscape measure, and the column anchors stay **left / center / right**. The jigsaw effect comes from varied vertical baselines within an otherwise predictable three-image group, not from changing the supplied order or making certain images arbitrarily dominant.

| Official group | Reading order | Vertical composition | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01–03 | Left → center → right | Center image drops slightly below its neighbors | Opens the field with a subtle interlock rather than a rigid shelf. |
| 04–06 | Left → center → right | Outside images drop; center image leads | Reverses the first group and keeps the eye moving diagonally. |
| 07–09 | Left → center → right | All three align on the lower baseline | Gives the first landscape group a clean, wide “breath.” |
| 10–12 | Left → center → right | Center image rises slightly | Creates the second landscape interlock without disturbing sequence. |
| 13–54 | Same left → center → right sequence | Repeat a four-group rhythm: center-low, outer-low, level, center-high | Keeps the composition varied yet learnable; every number remains in its existing group and left-to-right order. |

The offsets would be deliberately small—roughly **1.5–3rem**, never large enough to make one preview look misplaced or to break the existing 50% row cadence. Every triplet remains recognizable as a group. In effect, the negative space becomes the “puzzle” rather than the images becoming irregular shapes.

## Interaction and mobile treatment

On desktop, hovering an image continues to elevate it to **115%** of its normal scale, with a tight shadow and the registration marks moving independently by a few pixels. Because the base design is nearly frameless, this movement will feel more like bringing a print forward from an editorial layout than hovering over a product card.

On mobile, the jigsaw offsets disappear. Images return to a calm, single-column sequence in their exact numerical order, with a single small colored registration stroke as the only persistent accent. The existing darker, blurred lightbox backdrop and swipe navigation remain unchanged; they already create appropriate focus once an image is opened.

## Implementation boundaries

I would preserve the current 01–54 data order, existing portrait/landscape classifications, the full-resolution lightbox, keyboard navigation, desktop external arrows, mobile swipe, and the recently approved spacing and image sizes. The only structural change would be adding an explicit layout pattern to each existing triplet—such as `center-low`, `outer-low`, `level`, or `center-high`—and applying it at desktop width. I would also replace **“colour”** with **“color”** in the Studio paragraph.

## Approval question

Please confirm whether you want me to implement this **Measured Image Field** direction: a near-borderless, staggered editorial jigsaw with preserved group order and only the orange/cobalt registration marks as persistent visual accents.
