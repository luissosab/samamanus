# Contemporary Portfolio Composition Research

## Reviewed references

Graphéine’s Awwwards-featured portfolio is organized as a direct image-led index with clear filtering and rollover behavior, rather than relying on decorative image containers. Its project navigation demonstrates that the interaction can provide the polish while the image surface stays comparatively quiet. [Awwwards feature](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/portfolio-grapheine) and [live portfolio](https://grapheine.com/en/portfolio/).

The Awwwards-recognized Hotel Jägerhof reference identifies the “broken grid editorial layout” as an off-grid, magazine-style, asymmetrical composition. The important transferable principle is controlled asymmetry: images disrupt a regular grid, but the surrounding measure and spacing keep the page intentional rather than chaotic. [Awwwards feature](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/offgrid-layout-hotel-jagerhof).

The Awwwards gallery collection characterizes current gallery conventions as thumbnail grids, tiled mosaic layouts, and sequential carousels. A masonry collage is not inherently contemporary; the distinction comes from clear visual hierarchy, restraint, and how the image sequence remains legible. [Curated collection](https://www.awwwards.com/awwwards/collections/image-gallery-and-slideshows/).

Format’s survey of fashion portfolios highlights three relevant patterns: Floriane Lavrut’s masonry-style image field, Macey Rae Graham’s staggered layout with generous negative space, and Kiva Dam’s staggered collection layout. For Sama Knitwear, the most fitting principle is not a dense collage but a measured, staggered editorial field that protects image prominence and reading order. [Format survey](https://www.format.com/magazine/inspiring-fashion-design-portfolios).

Davide Baratta’s archive reinforces a second useful direction: a sparse archive environment with one distinct visual focal point, a restrained interface, and no conventional image-card styling. [Live archive](https://www.davidebaratta.com/archive/).

## Findings to apply

The proposed Sama Knitwear layout should remove the decorative picture-box convention altogether. Images should be treated as near-borderless archival plates; their only persistent graphic language should be slim orange and cobalt registration marks placed in the field adjacent to each image, not wrapped around it. A repeated three-column list should give way to a deliberately irregular but modular sequence, controlled by fixed column lines, baseline-aligned row bands, and predictable numerical continuity.
